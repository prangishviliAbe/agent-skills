# Copy

Read when: Writing or reviewing headlines, section text, calls to action, proof, or interface microcopy, in English or Georgian.

Generated copy is the fastest tell on a page, often before the visuals. It is built from phrases that fit any product, so it says nothing about this one. Words are design material: a specific headline removes the need for half the decoration around it.

## The specificity test

For every sentence: could a competitor, or a company in a different industry, paste it onto their page unchanged? If yes, it carries no information. Replace it with a statement only this product can make, or delete it.

| Generic | Specific |
| --- | --- |
| Seamless shipping solutions for the modern world | Parcels from Guangzhou to Tbilisi in 9 to 12 days, tracked at every transfer |
| Unlock the power of your data | See which invoices are overdue, and by how much, the moment you log in |
| Trusted by thousands of happy customers | Used by 214 importers in Georgia since 2019 (only if true and sourced) |
| Get started | Track a shipment |
| Powerful analytics, effortless insights | Weekly report: sales per shop, compared with the same week last year |
| We're passionate about quality | Every table is sanded by hand in our Kutaisi workshop; visit any weekday |

A specific sentence has a subject, a verb, and at least one of: a number, a name, a place, a time, a concrete object, or a consequence.

## Words and frames to remove

Remove on sight unless the sentence survives the specificity test without them.

```text
Verbs:      unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, supercharge, streamline, revolutionize,
            reimagine, transform, harness, leverage, delve, craft, curate
Adjectives: seamless, effortless, powerful, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation,
            world-class, innovative, intuitive, stunning, game-changing, best-in-class
Nouns:      solution, platform (as filler), journey, experience, ecosystem, synergy
Frames:     "It's not just X, it's Y"        "Where X meets Y"
            "X, reimagined"                  "Say goodbye to X"
            "Your all-in-one X for Y"        "Take your X to the next level"
            "Whether you're A or B"          "In today's fast-paced world"
            "Built for teams who..."         "The future of X is here"
            "X made simple"                  "Welcome to X"
Cadence:    three short fragments in a row ("Plan. Build. Ship.")
            every list having exactly three items
            a colon or dash reveal in every headline
            headline + subhead that says the same thing longer
```

Also remove: exclamation marks in interface text, emoji in headings and buttons, rhetorical questions as headings ("Tired of slow shipping?"), and the word "simply" before an instruction.

## Headlines

- Say what it is or what the visitor gets. Clever comes second and only if it is also clear.
- Use the audience's own words for the job. They search for "track parcel", not "logistics visibility".
- One idea. If it needs "and", it is two headlines or a headline and a subhead.
- The subhead adds the specific the headline lacked: how, for whom, how long, how much.
- Sentence case. No final period on a single-line headline; full punctuation on anything that reads as a sentence of prose.
- Length follows layout: check the wrap at every width, in every language.

## Calls to action

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Verb plus object, naming what happens next | "Calculate shipping cost", "Book a visit", "Download the price list" |
| One primary action per view | Secondary actions are plain links |
| The label matches the destination | A button saying "See pricing" lands on prices, not a contact form |
| No false urgency or pressure | No "Don't miss out", "Limited spots" unless literally true |
| Reassurance only when true and useful | "No card needed" belongs only where a card might be expected |

## Proof

Proof is true or absent. There is no third option.

- Testimonials: real person, full name, role, company, their actual words, with permission. Otherwise cut the section.
- Numbers: exact, sourced, dated. "214 importers" beats "200+", which beats an invented "10,000+".
- Logos: actual customers or partners, with permission.
- Ratings: link to the source where the rating lives.
- No real proof yet: show the product working, the price, the guarantee, the people behind it. A page without a testimonial section is normal; a page with a fake one is disqualifying.
- In mockups for a client who will supply content later, use realistic sample content and mark it visibly as sample so it cannot ship by accident.

## Interface microcopy

| Element | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Button | The action: "Save changes", "Delete 3 files" |
| Field label | A noun the user recognizes; hint text only for format or source ("From your receipt") |
| Error | What happened and how to fix it: "Enter the 10-digit code from your receipt" |
| Empty state | Why it is empty and the one action that fills it |
| Confirmation | The object and consequence: "Delete shipment GE-10482? This cannot be undone." |
| Success | What changed, briefly; no celebration for routine actions |
| Loading | What is happening if it takes long: "Checking customs status…" |

Use the same word for the same thing everywhere. If it is "shipment" in the navigation, it is not "parcel" in the table and "order" in the email.

## Sample data

Realistic sample data is part of the design. Use plausible local names, real-looking codes, uneven numbers, and varied lengths, including one long case that stresses the layout.

| Placeholder habit | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| John Doe, Jane Smith, Acme Corp | Names and companies plausible for the market, clearly sample |
| $1,000, 50%, 100 users | Uneven values: ₾1,284.50, 37%, 213 |
| Lorem ipsum | Draft real copy, even if rough; lorem hides layout problems |
| Every row "Active" | A realistic mix of states, including the awkward ones |

## Georgian copy

Translated-sounding Georgian is as recognizable as generated English. Write it as Georgian, not as a rendering of an English template.

| Calque or cliché | Natural, specific alternative |
| --- | --- |
| გახსენით თქვენი ბიზნესის პოტენციალი | Say what the product does: ამანათი გუანჯოუდან თბილისში 9–12 დღეში |
| აიყვანეთ თქვენი ბიზნესი ახალ დონეზე | State the concrete result with a number or example |
| ინოვაციური გადაწყვეტილებები, უმაღლესი ხარისხი | Name the thing and its measurable quality |
| თქვენი სანდო პარტნიორი | Give the reason to trust: years, volume, guarantee, address |
| დაიწყეთ ახლავე / გაიგეთ მეტი | Name the action: ამანათის მოძებნა, ტარიფების ნახვა |
| ყველაფერი ერთ პლატფორმაზე | List what is actually included |

Rules:

- Choose one form of address, formal თქვენ or informal შენ, from the brand's voice, and hold it across the whole product.
- Choose one pattern for action labels and keep it: verbal noun (ამანათის მოძებნა) or imperative (მოძებნეთ ამანათი). Mixing them looks careless.
- Georgian has no capital letters in everyday text. Do not imitate Title Case or all caps; hierarchy comes from size and weight.
- Use Georgian quotation marks „ “ and the en dash for ranges.
- Keep product terms consistent, and do not leave English words untranslated unless the audience really uses them.
- Georgian words and phrases are often longer than the English equivalents. Write the Georgian first when it is the primary language, and fit the layout to it.
- Have a native speaker read final copy when one is available; note it as Not run when not.

## Checks

| Check | Pass |
| --- | --- |
| Specificity | No sentence could move to a competitor unchanged |
| Banned words | None left without a concrete reason |
| Truth | Every claim, number, name, and logo is real or visibly marked as sample |
| Actions | Each button names what happens; one primary per view |
| Consistency | One term per concept, one form of address, one label pattern |
| Fit | Longest real strings in each language fit without clipping |
